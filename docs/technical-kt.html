Knowledge transfer · pacopt

Profit-Aware Campaign Optimizer

A bank has a £50K budget for a term-deposit campaign and 10,000 customers. The system decides whom to contact, and through which channel, to earn the most expected profit while following responsible-targeting rules.

Every figure on this page comes from the saved pipeline artifacts in artifacts/. All economics are illustrative assumptions on the public UCI Bank Marketing dataset, not bank performance.

£103.8Kexpected contribution of the GA list
+71%over random targeting (+£43.1K)
+14%over propensity-only targeting (+£12.5K)
100%of the proven MILP optimum
Engine 1

Customer Intelligence

Calibrated Logistic Regression gives P(subscribe). A propensity-by-value grid gives four business segments.

Engine 2

Profit Intelligence

The deposit is valued as a funding product. Expected profit EP = P × V − C.

Engine 3

Optimization Intelligence

A Genetic Algorithm picks the list under five kinds of constraint. An exact MILP proves the result optimal.

03Pipeline flow

  1. raw

    Load and validate

    data.load_raw checks the 17 columns, 45,211 rows, no nulls and a positive rate between 10% and 13%. data.clean lower-cases, maps "admin." to "admin", turns yes/no into 0/1 and keeps "unknown" as its own category.

  2. M1

    Features and split

    build_features adds RFM-inspired features and eligibility flags. IDs are C100001 onwards in original row order. A 70/30 split stratified on y (seed 42) gives train 31,647 and test 13,564. The universe is a stratified 10,000 drawn from the test set.

  3. M2

    build_problem keeps the 9,041 eligible customers. Random ×50, Propensity, Greedy and Rule-based run through one shared engine, then the GA and the MILP. check_constraints audits every list independently.

  6. out

    Consumers

    Streamlit dashboard, notebooks 01 to 05, MLflow runs.

04Data and features

4.1 Dataset

  • UCI Bank Marketing bank-full.csv: Portugal, 2008–2010, CC BY 4.0.
  • Target y (subscribed to a term deposit) has a base rate of 11.7%.
  • bank-additional was rejected because it has no balance column, and balance is the monetary proxy (D-003).

4.2 Leakage policy (D-004, D-023)

Four columns are forbidden as features. The feature list is an allow-list (MODEL_FEATURES), and assert_no_leakage() runs at import time, at fit time and in parametrised tests.

ColumnWhy it leaks
durationCall length is only known after the call. A "yes" means a long call.
campaignContacts in this campaign. Customers who say yes stop being called, so the outcome partly causes the value.
day, monthReflect the bank's historical calling waves, not customer traits.

Ablation: ROC-AUC is 0.753 for the chosen model, 0.776 with campaign/day/month added and 0.908 with duration added. The model gives up 0.15 AUC on purpose so it can be used before a real campaign.

An allow-list fails safe: a new derived column stays out until someone adds it on purpose. A deny-list would let it in automatically.

4.3 The 15 model features

Numeric (8): age, balance_slog, frequency, never_contacted, prior_success, housing, loan, default.
Categorical (7): job, marital, education, contact, poutcome, age_band, recency_bucket.

  • default is a model feature even though customers in default are excluded from targeting. The model is trained on everyone; eligibility is applied at decision time.
  • Non-model columns kept: raw balance (economics), balance_quintile (RFM M score), recency_days.
  • "RFM-inspired, not true RFM": there are no transactions. R is time since the last campaign contact, F is previous campaign contacts, and M is the balance stock, never revenue.

4.4 Eligibility and responsible targeting (D-007)

Excluding by age outright would itself be discrimination, so the rule caps and flags instead. The eligible universe is 9,041 of 10,000 (90.4%). Every rule can be toggled in the dashboard sidebar.

4.5 Split (D-014, D-024)

The 70/30 stratified split gives train 31,647 and test 13,564. The universe is a stratified sample of 10,000 taken from the test set, so every score that drives a money decision is out-of-sample and the backtest stays honest.

05Propensity models

5.1 Logistic Regression (primary)

Pipeline[ ColumnTransformer( StandardScaler(numeric),
                             OneHotEncoder(handle_unknown="ignore", dense)(categorical) ),
          LogisticRegression(max_iter=5000) ]                  # lbfgs, L2
GridSearchCV  C ∈ {0.003, 0.01, 0.03, 0.1, 0.3, 1, 3, 10}
              StratifiedKFold(5, shuffle, seed 42), scoring = average_precision
              → best C = 0.3, CV PR-AUC = 0.374
CalibratedClassifierCV(clone(best_pipeline), method="isotonic", cv=StratifiedKFold(5))

Maths. p = σ(β₀ + βᵀx), with σ(z) = 1 / (1 + e⁻ᶻ). scikit-learn minimises:

½‖β‖²  +  C · Σᵢ [ −yᵢ log pᵢ − (1 − yᵢ) log(1 − pᵢ) ]
  • C is the inverse regularisation strength. A small C means a stronger penalty.
  • Odds ratio = eβ. For a scaled numeric feature it is the change in odds per 1 standard deviation.
  • One-hot with no level dropped: intercept and dummies are collinear, and L2 makes the problem identifiable. Each level is read against the regularised average, not a reference level.
  • No class_weight="balanced": re-weighting shifts the intercept and inflates every probability. P is multiplied by money in EP, so it must be calibrated, not just well ranked.
  • Tuned on PR-AUC, not ROC-AUC: with an 11.7% positive class and decisions at the top of the list, ROC-AUC is dominated by easy negatives that are never contacted. Average precision AP = Σₙ (Rₙ − Rₙ₋₁) · Pₙ weights the high-score region.

5.2 Isotonic calibration (D-027)

  • Problem: the raw LR's top decile predicted 39.8% and observed 43.8%, a 3.96 pp gap. That decile is dominated by prior-success customers, where recency and outcome interact in a way a linear model can't express.
  • Isotonic regression fits a non-decreasing step function f minimising Σ(yᵢ − f(sᵢ))². It is solved by Pool Adjacent Violators: scan scores in order and merge adjacent blocks that break monotonicity into their mean.
  • Platt scaling, f(s) = σ(a·s + b), was tested and failed, because the error isn't sigmoid-shaped.
  • Result: max decile gap 3.96 → 1.22 pp, ROC-AUC unchanged at 0.753.
Interview subtlety

CalibratedClassifierCV(cv=5) with the default ensemble=True trains five (LR, isotonic) pairs. Each LR is fitted on 4 folds and its calibrator on the fifth, and predictions average the five. The explanation model lr_explain is a separate uncalibrated LR refit on the full training set. "Isotonic is monotone, so the coefficients still explain the scorer" is therefore approximately true, not exactly. Isotonic also creates ties (plateaus).

5.3 Propensity cap at 0.90 (D-028)

Isotonic tails are fitted on very few points. On the test set, 8 customers had a mean predicted P of 97.3% but converted at 62.5%. The fix is propensity = clip(p, upper=0.90). It only lowers scores and affects 7 universe customers. Without it the optimiser would always pick them on overstated EP. The rank uses the uncapped score.

5.4 Random Forest (challenger)

  • 300 trees, min_samples_leaf=50, max_features="sqrt", same preprocessing (scaling is harmless for trees), isotonic calibration with cv=5. Not tuned: it is a benchmark.
  • Maths: bagging (bootstrap samples) plus random feature subsets at each split, Gini impurity 1 − Σpₖ². P is the average over trees of each leaf's positive fraction. Averaging pulls probabilities towards the middle, so RF probabilities need calibration.
  • Five calibration folds × 300 trees = 1,500 trees, which is most of the 30 MB model.joblib.

07Profit economics

A term deposit is a liability, a source of funding. The bank never earns the principal. It earns the funds-transfer-pricing (FTP) spread.

D_i  = clip(D_min + α·max(balance_i, 0), D_min, D_max)    = clip(10,000 + balance, 10,000, 100,000)
V_i  = D_i · (FTP − customer_rate) · term − incentive − onboarding
     = D_i · 0.0175 · 1 − 40 − 5                           → V ∈ [£130, £1,705]
C_i  = channel cost: mobile £12 · landline £10 · digital £2   (channel fixed by `contact`)
BE_i = max(P_i − P0, 0) · V_i        break-even contact cost
EP_i = BE_i − C_i                    expected incremental profit
  • Decision rule: contact i only if Pᵢ · Vᵢ > Cᵢ, i.e. Pᵢ > Cᵢ / Vᵢ. At the minimum V (£130) the threshold is 9.2% for a mobile call and only 1.5% for digital, which is why almost all digital customers have positive EP.
  • P0, the organic rate: the data holds only contacted customers and has no control group, so P0 defaults to 0. "Incremental" means relative to the baseline strategies, not causal uplift (D-015).
  • Fully-loaded channel costs (D-009): at the brief's £5 per contact, £50K buys the whole universe and the budget never forces a choice.
  • Sensitivity: the funding spread dominates. A 25% lower spread roughly halves positive EP. economics.sensitivity() scales spread, incentive or contact cost across ×0.5 to ×1.5.

Calibration of the defaults (D-031)

The plan's starting values (D_min £5K, incentive £15) meant the budget never bound: all positive-EP customers cost only about £33K to contact. With D_min £10K and incentive £40:

  • 6,700 of 9,041 eligible customers (74%) have positive EP.
  • Their contact cost after channel caps is £54.3K, above the £50K budget, so the budget binds.
  • Total positive EP is £104K, and the top 10% of customers hold 58% of it.
  • The median eligible customer is worth about £11 of contact; the best is worth up to £525.
Expect this challenge

The assumptions were tuned so the optimisation problem is non-trivial. Defend it as a documented, editable and tested scenario-design choice, not as a result.

08Optimisation

8.1 Formal problem

max_x  Σ_i x_i · EP_i                         x_i ∈ {0,1},  i ∈ eligible (9,041)
s.t.   Σ x_i · C_i              ≤ 50,000      budget
       Σ x_i                    ≤ 6,000       max contacts
       Σ_{i ∈ calls}   x_i      ≤ 4,500       call capacity (mobile + landline)
       Σ_{i ∈ digital} x_i      ≤ 2,500       digital capacity
       n_s / N                  ≤ 0.60        every segment s
       n_Growing / N ≥ 0.15 ;  n_Dormant / N ≥ 0.10
       n_75+ / N                ≤ 0.02        protected cap

This is a 0-1 multidimensional knapsack with ratio constraints, NP-hard in general.

Linearising a share constraint: n_s / N ≤ s becomes n_s − s·N ≤ 0, which is Σᵢ xᵢ · (mᵢ − s) ≤ 0 where mᵢ = 1 if customer i is in the segment. It is linear in x, so it goes straight into the MILP.

8.2 The shared constraint engine (D-035)

Every heuristic is just a priority order passed through one vectorised _Engine, so strategies differ only in targeting, never in how loosely they handle constraints. The engine works on a boolean matrix X (rows = solutions, columns = customers), which is what makes the GA fast. repair() runs these steps in order:

  1. caps: channel capacities, then max contacts, then budget. Within each limit it keeps the first k customers in priority order, using a cumsum trick in _top().
  2. max_shares: for an over-represented segment, drop d = ⌈(nₛ − s·N) / (1 − s)⌉ members. Derived from (nₛ − d) / (N − d) ≤ s. The same applies to the 75+ cap.
  3. min_shares: add a = ⌈(s·N − nₛ) / (1 − s)⌉ of the best unselected members. Negative-EP customers may be added if a minimum requires it; 75+ customers never are. Hard caps are then restored with minimum-segment members locked. If a segment runs out of members, the rest of the list shrinks.
  4. fill: top up spare capacity in priority order, first a bulk prefix, then single additions (up to 256 rounds), skipping anyone who would break a limit. Share caps are measured against n_ref, the expected final list size, so the first customer isn't "100% of the list".
  5. Repeat up to 4 times until violation() == 0.
  • Two priority orders: budget_order where money is the limit and count_order where a head-count is the limit. The GA uses EP/C for money and EP for head-count. This matters (see 8.6).
  • violation() is in customer-equivalent units: budget overrun ÷ minimum cost, plus excess contacts, excess per channel and share shortfalls or overshoots.
  • check_constraints() is an independent auditor. It uses only the campaign table and customer IDs, returns one row per constraint, and also checks IDs are unique, known and eligible.

8.4 Genetic Algorithm (D-036)

ParameterValueMeaning
ChromosomeBinaryOne gene per customer in the gene pool: positive-EP customers plus all Growing and Dormant members (the segments with minimums).
Population100
Generations / patience300 / 50Stops after 50 generations without improvement.
SelectionTournament, k = 3Each parent is the best of 3 drawn at random, with replacement.
CrossoverUniform, rate 0.9Each gene from either parent with probability 0.5. 10% of children copy parent 1.
MutationAsymmetric bit-flipAbout 30 genes switched on (probability 30 / n_free per gene) and about 0.5 switched off per child.
Elitism2% (2 individuals)The best never gets worse (monotone, tested).
Seeding4Greedy list plus 3 perturbed copies (drop 2%, add 0.2%). The other 96 are random at the density the budget allows.
Repairrepair(fill=True)Every evaluated child is feasible.
FitnessΣEP − penalty · violationPenalty = 10 · max|EP|. A safety net that repair should make unused.
Time limitNone / 30 sNone in the pipeline, 30 s in the dashboard.
  • Why asymmetric mutation works: add about 30 customers, and repair drops the weakest. The net effect is a set of swaps that test new customers. With symmetric 5-on / 5-off, random drops lost good customers, children were almost always worse and the search stalled.
  • Tournament selection pressure: with replacement, the individual of rank r (1 = best) in a population of N wins a k-tournament with probability [(N − r + 1)ᵏ − (N − r)ᵏ] / Nᵏ. Larger k means stronger pressure.
  • Run result: 90 generations in 12.7 s, reaching £103,793.08, exactly the MILP optimum. It reaches the optimum in 10 of 10 runs (5 seeds, seeded and unseeded).

8.5 MILP

  • scipy.optimize.milp with the HiGHS solver (branch-and-cut on LP relaxations). It minimises −EP.
  • Sparse CSR constraint matrix. Time limit 180 s in the pipeline (120 s in the dashboard), mip_rel_gap = 1e-6.
  • Solves over all 9,041 eligible customers, which proves the GA's gene-pool restriction loses nothing.
  • Result: proven optimal (HiGHS status 7, gap 0) in 28.7 s.

Why the GA beats Greedy by £41

At the optimum the budget does not bind (£47.4K < £50K). The 6,000-contact cap binds instead. In Lagrangian terms, maximise Σxᵢ(EPᵢ − λCᵢ − μ). With a slack budget λ = 0, so the right ranking is by EP, not EP/C.

  • Greedy ranks by EP/C and fills all 2,500 cheap digital slots.
  • The optimum swaps 100 low-EP digital contacts for 100 higher-EP calls.
  • Ratio-greedy is optimal only for the fractional single-constraint knapsack (Dantzig). With several interacting constraints, no fixed ranking rule is optimal.
  • The GA's ROI is lower than Greedy's because the objective is total profit, not ROI. It spends £1K more to earn £41 more, which is still positive marginal profit.
Say this plainly

In the default scenario most of the gain comes from ranking by profit per £. The GA's advantage grows when constraints interact:

  • Few call agents: GA +£3.5K over Greedy
  • Balanced segment mix: +£1.6K
  • Contact costs halved: +£2.7K (the contact cap binds before the budget, and Greedy even falls behind Propensity)

10MLOps and dashboard

MLflow

  • Local store: sqlite mlruns.db, artifacts in mlartifacts/.
  • propensity-model: parent run with child runs for LR and RF (params, metrics, permutation importance, coefficients, model with signature). The selected model is registered as propensity-champion.
  • campaign-optimization: parent run with a child run per strategy, including the constraint checklist. The GA run logs per-generation fitness as stepped metrics.
  • data-drift: one run per click on the Monitoring page.
  • The @_safe decorator means tracking can never break the pipeline; failures become warnings.

Evidently

  • Drift is simulated: there is no production feed. Reference = 3,000 training rows. Current = 3,000 universe rows with 7 drift sliders: age shift, balance %, unknown contact %, prior success %, contacted-before %, job-mix shift, loan stress.
  • apply_drift recomputes the derived features (age_band, balance_slog, prior_success, recency) so the columns stay consistent.
  • Reports: DataDriftPreset, prediction drift (ColumnDriftMetric on the propensity score) and DataQualityPreset.
  • Evidently 0.4 picks the statistical test by column type and sample size. For over 1,000 rows it normally uses normed Wasserstein distance for numeric columns and Jensen-Shannon distance for categorical ones; dataset drift is declared when at least half the columns drift. Check the per-column test field rather than quoting from memory.
  • Tech debt: Evidently is pinned below 0.5 (newer versions need plotly < 6), and the code restores a pandas helper that pandas 3 removed.

Dashboard architecture (D-038)

  • Scenario is a frozen dataclass of economics, eligibility, constraints and model choice. Its JSON is the st.cache_data key.
  • Fast path, about 2 s: the campaign table and four baselines recompute on every slider change.
  • Slow path: GA (30 s cap) and MILP (120 s) run only on a button press.
  • Fingerprint: SHA-256 over customer IDs, EP, cost, channel group, segment, protected flag and the constraint JSON. Saved GA/MILP results are shown only when it matches, so stale numbers never appear.
  • Persistent p_* session keys are mirrored into per-page widget keys s_*, because Streamlit drops widget state when you leave a page.

12Limitations

Modelling and data

  1. Not causal. Everyone in the data was contacted, so P is P(subscribe | contacted). EP overstates true incremental value for customers who would subscribe anyway.
  2. Random split on time-ordered data. bank-full runs from May 2008 to November 2010, across the financial crisis. A random split mixes periods, so metrics are likely optimistic against a forward-in-time deployment.
  3. The calibrated scorer is a 5-model ensemble, while explanations come from a separate refit LR. Isotonic plateaus create ties.
  4. The RF challenger is untuned, and the 0.02 switch threshold is fixed rather than a statistical test.
  5. 2008–2010 Portuguese data, euros shown as £. No transactions, so RFM is only inspired.

Economics

  1. All values are illustrative and the spread dominates. Defaults were chosen so the budget binds, and it binds with only about 9% headroom.
  2. Deposit size is assumed to be balance + £10K, with no data behind it.
  3. Maximising expected value is risk-neutral: it ignores variance and correlated outcomes.

Optimisation

  1. In the default scenario the GA adds £41 over Greedy, and the MILP is fast enough to use directly. The GA's real value is for non-linear objectives, which aren't modelled yet.
  2. Single-shot campaign: no contact policy over time, no response saturation or fatigue, no repeated attempts.
  3. Segment shares are count-based, not spend-based.

Engineering and scale

  1. Streamlit is single-user, and the GA blocks the page while it runs.
  2. The joblib model is pickled (tied to library versions, unsafe from untrusted sources) and about 30 MB.
  3. Windows-specific threading backend; Evidently pinned with a pandas patch; drift only simulated.
  4. The fairness monitor cannot fire at a 66% selection rate.
  5. No CI. The reproducibility check reuses the local environment instead of a fresh uv sync.
  6. The GA population matrix is 100 × n booleans. Fine at n ≈ 7K, but at a million customers it is about 100 MB per matrix plus temporary copies. The MILP's LP relaxation is nearly integral (few constraints, many variables), so LP-plus-rounding or Lagrangian methods scale well.

14Interview Q&A

Use quiz mode to hide the answers, try each question out loud, then open it to check. Tick the ones you can answer cold; ticks are saved in this browser only.

0 / 27 known
Q1Why drop campaign when most notebooks only drop duration?▶

Customers who say yes stop being called, so the outcome partly causes the value, and it isn't known before the campaign. The ablation shows +0.023 AUC if it's added.

Q2Why does calibration matter here?▶

EP = P · V − C. A 4 pp under-statement in the top decile underprices exactly the customers you most want to contact.

Q3Why isotonic and not Platt scaling?▶

Platt was tested and failed: the miscalibration wasn't sigmoid-shaped. Isotonic is non-parametric and monotone, solved with Pool Adjacent Violators.

Q4What are the risks of isotonic calibration?▶

It overfits the sparse tails and produces a step function with ties. Hence the 0.90 cap and fitting it with 5-fold CV.

Q5Why tune on PR-AUC?▶

Imbalanced classes (11.7% positive) and decisions at the top of the list. ROC-AUC is inflated by easy negatives that are never contacted.

Q6What does C = 0.3 mean?▶

C is the inverse L2 regularisation strength, so 0.3 is moderate regularisation. It was selected by 5-fold stratified CV on average precision.

Q7Why not use class_weight="balanced"?▶

It shifts the intercept and inflates P, which breaks the money maths in EP.

Q8Why did the Random Forest lose?▶

+0.016 PR-AUC, below the pre-agreed 0.02 threshold, and worse calibration (1.98 vs 1.22 pp).

Q9Explain expected profit.▶

The bank earns the FTP spread on the deposit, minus incentive and onboarding: that is V. Weight it by P (less the organic rate P0) and subtract the channel contact cost C.

Q10Why isn't the deposit principal counted as revenue?▶

A deposit is a liability. The revenue is the funding benefit, the spread between the FTP rate and the customer rate.

Q11When is a contact worthwhile?▶

When P > C / V. Digital at £2 needs only about 1.5% propensity at the minimum value; a mobile call at £12 needs about 9.2%.

Q12Why is Greedy (EP/C) near-optimal, and when does it fail?▶

It is optimal for the fractional knapsack with one budget constraint. It fails when a head-count cap binds (then rank by EP) or when several constraints interact.

Q13Why use a GA if the MILP solves in 29 seconds?▶

The brief required one, and it extends to non-linear objectives (diminishing returns, channel interactions) that a MILP can't express directly. The MILP keeps the GA's claim honest.

Q14How do you guarantee the GA is never worse than Greedy?▶

The Greedy list is one of the seeds, and elitism keeps the best individual every generation.

Q15How are segment-share constraints linearised?▶

n_s / N ≤ s becomes Σᵢ xᵢ (mᵢ − s) ≤ 0, where mᵢ marks segment membership. Minimums use ≥ 0 with the same form.

Q16Derive the repair drop count for a max-share limit.▶

(n − d) / (N − d) ≤ s gives n − d ≤ sN − sd, so d(1 − s) ≥ n − sN and d ≥ (n − sN) / (1 − s), rounded up.

Q17Why asymmetric mutation?▶

Adding about 30 genes and letting repair drop the weakest works as a set of swaps. Symmetric flips (5 on / 5 off) destroyed fitness and the search stalled.

Q18How do you know every strategy is feasible?▶

An independent check_constraints that uses only customer IDs and the campaign table, plus property tests over 35 random scenarios.

Q19Is the +71% causal?▶

No. It is relative to a random-targeting baseline under the same model. A causal claim needs a randomised hold-out and uplift modelling.

Q20What does the backtest prove?▶

That P is well calibrated on the selected lists (891 expected vs 889 actual conversions). It does not prove a campaign effect, because everyone in the data was contacted.

Q21Why are 75+ customers capped rather than excluded?▶

Blanket exclusion by age is itself discriminatory. A 2% cap plus human review applies extra care without denying access.

Q22Why can't the fairness flag fire?▶

At 66% selection, the maximum possible ratio is 1 / 0.66 ≈ 1.51, below the 2× threshold. Share-of-list vs share-of-eligible is the better guide.

Q23Why build the universe from the test set?▶

So every score that drives a money decision is out-of-sample, which keeps the comparison and the backtest honest.

Q24What dominates the result's sensitivity?▶

The funding spread: −25% roughly halves positive EP. It is a Treasury input and shouldn't be tuned to hit a target.

Q25How does the dashboard avoid showing stale optimiser results?▶

Saved GA/MILP results carry a fingerprint of the problem. They are shown only when the current scenario's fingerprint matches; otherwise the page shows the baselines and a Run GA button.

Q26What is the weakest part of the project?▶

Causality (no control group) and illustrative economics tuned so the budget binds. The next steps are a randomised hold-out and Treasury inputs.

Q27How would you scale to 5 million customers?▶

LP relaxation plus rounding or Lagrangian decomposition (few constraints, many variables), batch scoring in a distributed job, and the GA only on a pre-filtered candidate set.
